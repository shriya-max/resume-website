Read the poem: I Lied to Be Liked

I lied, not because I loved deception,
But because I feared rejection.
I feared the eyes that might disapprove,
So I became someone easier to love.

I concealed the flaws I couldn’t embrace,
And wore a borrowed smile upon my face.
I changed my stories, disguised my truth,
Hoping to be accepted, if only for a moment or two.

But lies are peculiar; they multiply,
One becomes ten beneath a borrowed disguise.
Until the truth, once buried deep inside,
Finds its way to the surface, no matter how hard we hide.

And now I wonder, must I wear this shame?
Will one mistake forever define my name?
Will all that I am disappear from view,
Because of the things I was too afraid to pursue?

Perhaps the judgment I fear the most
Is merely a shadow I have raised like a ghost.

But this was never truly about them;
It was about the person I buried within.
My greatest battle was never for their affection,
But against my own insecurities and imperfections.

I have truths to face, mistakes to own,
And a version of myself yet to be known.
I cannot undo the person I’ve been,
But I can choose who I become from within.

I lied to be liked,
But I am learning to live without disguise.

For I spent so long trying to become someone worth loving,
That I forgot—

I was worthy of it all along.
